Product patterns

Common operational problems should have familiar solutions.

Dashboard

Context, status, important metrics, exceptions, and the next useful actions.

List + filters

Search, saved filters, status, bulk selection, clear columns, and row actions.

Detail workspace

Persistent context, summary, tabs, actions, history, and related records.

Settings

Grouped decisions, explanatory copy, sensible defaults, and explicit save state.

Wizard

Staged complexity, progress, validation near the source, review, and confirmation.

Empty state

Meaning, value, next action, and optional learning path—not merely “No data.”

Table workflow

Responsive data density, sorting, selection, inline status, and safe bulk actions.

Modal + drawer

Modals confirm focused decisions. Drawers support contextual editing without losing place.

System feedback

Inline validation, notices, progress, toast confirmation, warnings, and recoverable errors.

Composition rules

Patterns are guidance, not templates to repeat blindly.

RuleReason
Start with the user’s current mental state.The same components should be ordered differently for discovery, setup, management, and troubleshooting.
Do not stack identical section structures.Alternating weight, media, utility, and whitespace keeps long experiences understandable.
Keep marketing and product density distinct.Marketing creates understanding; product interfaces support repeated work.
Reuse behavior before appearance.A familiar save, filter, error, or confirmation behavior matters more than decorative sameness.
