Experience rules

Rules keep the system coherent when no designer is in the room.

These rules translate Rally's foundations into practical decisions for marketing pages, WordPress interfaces, add-ons, documentation, forms, and communication.

Hierarchy

Every screen needs one clear job.

1
One primary action

Secondary actions may support it, but the page should never ask users to choose between several equally loud next steps.

2
Reveal complexity gradually

Use defaults, summaries, sections, and staged workflows rather than presenting every option at once.

3
Keep context visible

Users should know which event, season, team, player, budget, or evaluation they are changing.

4
State consequences before commitment

Publishing, deleting, charging, scheduling, and eligibility changes require clear previews and confirmation language.

Writing

Prefer “Create event,” “Publish schedule,” and “Save changes.” Avoid “Submit,” “Proceed,” and vague “Continue” labels.

Feedback

Success is quiet and specific. Errors explain what happened, preserve user input, and provide a recovery step.

Empty states

Explain what belongs here, why it matters, and what the user can do next. Never stop at “No data.”

Visual discipline

Restraint creates trust.
