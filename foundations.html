Foundations

How Rally should feel before deciding how it should look.

Foundations are the permanent ideas beneath the changing interface. They guide the website, software, documentation, support, emails, and every future Rally product.

Brand foundation

Rally supports the people who build communities.

Mission

Empower organizers to spend less time managing logistics and more time building communities.

Brand promise

Make organizing sports feel less like administrative work and more like building community.

Experience principles

Five principles shape every interaction.

01

Calm over clever

Design to reduce stress, not to demonstrate how inventive the interface can be.

02

Progress over perfection

Help organizers move forward with clear defaults, staged decisions, and visible next steps.

03

Explain, don't surprise

Users should understand important consequences before an action occurs.

04

Organizers first

When convenience for the system conflicts with clarity for the organizer, the organizer wins.

05

Relationships before transactions

Support, onboarding, and sales should feel like a partnership rather than a ticket queue.

Personality

Calm confidence

Experienced, organized, warm, practical, reliable, and quietly ambitious.

Voice

Explain, don't impress

Use everyday language, active voice, short sentences, and specific next steps.

Restraint

Never salesy

Avoid hype, fake urgency, inflated claims, buzzwords, and celebration that overwhelms the task.

Photography

Real community moments carry the soul of Rally.

Prioritize organizers, volunteers, setup, check-in, conversation, empty gyms before doors open, and people enjoying a community someone worked hard to build.

  • Use authentic event photography.
  • Show the work around the game, not only game action.
  • Use product UI as the secondary visual language.
  • Avoid generic offices, posed handshakes, and headset support imagery.
REAL PEOPLE
organizers · volunteers · players
REAL PLACES
gyms · courts · check-in tables

Accessibility is foundational

Readable type, clear hierarchy, high contrast, keyboard access, visible focus, descriptive labels, reduced motion, and understandable errors are product quality—not optional polish.

The Rally Test

  • Does this reduce work for organizers?
  • Is the next step obvious?
  • Is the language calm and helpful?
  • Does it create trust rather than hype?
  • Would it make sense to a first-time organizer?
